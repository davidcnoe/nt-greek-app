No remains of the parent Indo-Furopean tongue are extant, but,
by means of comparative linguistics, scholars have been able to
reconstruct a large part of its vocabulary and grammar.

The method and validity of comparative linguistics can be
illustrated within one branch of the Indo-European family. The
Romance languages are obviously related because it can he ob-
served that, within historic times, they have assumed their
present forms in developing from their common source, the latin
language. Thus, for example, the latin word caballus, meaning
‘a pack-horse, a nag,’ is the origin of all Romance words for
‘horse,’ such as French cheval, Spanish cahallo, Italian caval-
lo, Portuguese cavallo, Rumanian cal, Provencal and Catalan,
cavall.® So, too, when various extant Indo-European languages
are compared, the hypothetical parent tongue may he reconstruc-
ted with a considerable degree of probability. Thus, the fact
that the Greek word μήτηρ resembles the Sanskrit mAtar-, Aves-
ten (Old Persian) matd, Old Armenian mair, Latin mAter, Old High
German muoter (modern German Nutter), O1d Irish mthir, Old
Slavic mati, etc., renders it highly probable that all these

words have come from an Indo-European word *m&tér-."7
