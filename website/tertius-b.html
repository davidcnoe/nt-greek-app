We at LatinPerDiem are pleased to announce Tertius: The NT Greek Vocab Trainer, an app that gets you ready to read the New Testament in Greek. If you learned some Koine or Attic Greek once and want to read the New Testament without reaching for the English, Tertius builds the vocabulary that stands between you and the text.

Developed by Dr. David C. Noe and the LatinPerDiem team, Tertius brings the words of the New Testament to your phone, tablet, or computer. Since 2015, LatinPerDiem has delivered manageable, context-rich Latin and Greek lessons to a worldwide audience through its YouTube channel (now more than 2,500 free videos). Our philosophy is simple: people learn languages best through real use in context rather than isolated abstractions.

Learn the Words the New Testament Actually Uses

Tertius teaches the 1,287 words of Bruce Metzger's Lexical Aids for Students of New Testament Greek, most frequent first, with each word's root family woven in, so a new word is learned alongside the words from which it is built. As you master words, Tertius shows how much of the New Testament you now know: the share of all the running words in the text covered by the words you have learned. The 28 commonest words already cover half of the New Testament.

Features

  • Metzger's 1,287 New Testament words, ordered by frequency, with root families woven in
  • A session-based memory system: a word is mastered once you answer it correctly on the first try in five sessions on five different days, and mastered words come back at growing intervals for review
  • Metzger's glosses, with his English derivatives as memory aids for 441 words ("Cf. apostasy")
  • Souter's Pocket Lexicon to the Greek New Testament built in, searchable in Greek letters, Latin letters, or English
  • MossMethod meanings: if you have studied MossMethod for Greek, Tertius shows Moss's meaning for the 625 words in his First Greek Reader and counts them toward your progress
  • Readings beyond the New Testament: eleven passages with H. P. V. Nunn's notes, chiefly early Christian (the Didache, Hermas, Clement, the martyrdoms of Polycarp and Ignatius, Diognetus), with Plato and Lucian besides, coloured by what you have mastered, every word tappable, and a public-domain English translation one tap away
  • Diacriticals that change meaning: a quiz on 18 sets of words spelled alike but for an accent or breathing (εἰ and εἶ, ἤ and ἥ, οὐ and οὗ), each in a verse of the New Testament
  • Your choice of pace (3, 5, 8, or 12 new words per session), answer mode (recall or multiple choice), and direction (Greek to English, English to Greek, or both)
  • Share your progress with a study partner or group [CONFIRM WHAT SHIPS AT LAUNCH]
  • Works offline; iOS, Android, and the web [CONFIRM PLATFORMS]

Getting the Most from the App

  • Train daily. About 30 minutes per day, five days per week. Several short sessions fit in that time.
  • Recall before you look. After a first look at a new word, Tertius asks for its meaning before showing it. Retrieving a word from memory fixes it far better than looking at it again.
  • Watch the share of the New Testament. At 85%, you know the words behind more than eight of every ten words in the text, and daily reading of the Greek New Testament becomes realistic.
  • Then read. The point of Tertius is to get you into the text. Join The Greek New Testament in a Year, or start Matthew 1 on your own.

Whom It's For

Tertius is built for readers who already know some Greek: pastors and seminary graduates whose Greek has rusted, seminary students, MossMethod students and graduates, teachers, and lifelong learners who want to read the New Testament in the original. True beginners should start with a first-year course, such as MossMethod for Greek.
