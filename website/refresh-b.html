I, Tertius

Near the end of Romans, after Paul's long list of greetings, another voice breaks in: ἀσπάζομαι ὑμᾶς ἐγὼ Τέρτιος ὁ γράψας τὴν ἐπιστολὴν ἐν κυρίῳ (Rom. 16:22): “I Tertius, who took down this letter, greet you in the Lord.”

Tertius was the scribe who wrote the letter down. Paul often worked this way: he dictated, and at the end took the pen to add a greeting in his own hand (1 Cor. 16:21; Gal. 6:11; Col. 4:18; 2 Thess. 3:17). Every word of Romans passed through Tertius's ear and onto his page, in Greek, before anyone in Rome read a line of it.

Tertius wrote out one book of the New Testament. In a year, you will read all twenty-seven.
